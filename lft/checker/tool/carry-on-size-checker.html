How to measure your carry-on properly

Most gate-check disasters happen because travellers measure their bag empty, then pack it tight and expand the dimensions by an inch in every direction. Here's how to measure for a result that actually matches what happens at the gate.

Measure the bag fully packed

Pack the bag exactly as you intend to fly with it — clothes, toiletries, electronics, the lot. A soft-sided bag can grow 1–2 inches once packed. A hardside spinner won't, but its handles and wheels still count.

Include wheels, handles, side pockets and bumpers

Airlines measure the total external footprint of the bag, including all protrusions. Bag manufacturers often quote "internal" or "body-only" dimensions to look more competitive. Always measure the outside, from wheel tip to handle top, and pocket-to-pocket on the sides.

Strict airlines: leave a safety margin

Ryanair, Wizz Air, and Spirit (pre-shutdown) are notorious for gate-checking bags that sit a centimetre over the limit. If you're flying one of these carriers, aim for at least 1 cm under the published size on every axis. A bag that fits the sizer at home may not fit the sizer at the gate.

Weigh everything together if your airline enforces combined weight

Several airlines — Emirates, Qatar, Singapore, most Asian carriers, and some EU low-cost carriers — enforce a combined carry-on + personal item weight limit (often 7–10 kg). Weigh both bags on a luggage scale before leaving for the airport.

Quick answers

Does fare type change my allowance?

Yes. On many airlines a cheaper fare includes only a personal item (under-seat bag), while a standard or upgraded fare includes a full carry-on in the overhead locker.

Do wheels and handles count towards the size?

Yes. Measure the full external size including wheels, handles, bumpers and any bulging pockets.

What if the airline weighs cabin bags?

Enter your bag weight as well as dimensions. Airlines such as Ryanair, Wizz Air, Emirates, Qatar and most Asian carriers enforce strict 7–10 kg cabin bag limits.

Should I still check with the airline directly?

Yes. This tool is for quick pre-travel checking, but airlines can and do change policies. Always verify your specific fare allowance before you travel.

Why does the checker sort dimensions rather than compare length-to-length?

Because you can rotate a bag to fit. The checker uses best-fit logic: it sorts your bag's three dimensions and the airline's three limits from largest to smallest, then compares them — the fairest real-world test.

Does this size checker work for international flights?

Yes. The tool covers 51 airlines across North America, Europe, the Middle East, Asia, Oceania, and Latin America. Each airline's rules reflect their published international and domestic policies. Some airlines apply different limits on international routes — the tool flags this where relevant.

What's the difference between a personal item and a carry-on?

A personal item is a smaller bag (typically 17–18 inches max) that fits under the seat in front of you — a laptop bag, small backpack, or purse. A carry-on is a larger bag (typically 22 inches max) that goes in the overhead bin. Most fares include both. Basic economy fares on US carriers often include only a personal item.

Can I take a backpack and a carry-on together?

Usually yes — if the backpack qualifies as a personal item (fits under the seat). If your backpack is a 40L+ travel pack, it'll likely count as your full carry-on and you won't be allowed a second bag unless you've paid for one. The carry-on size checker treats large backpacks as carry-ons, not personal items.

Does this tool include weight limits?

Yes. The carry-on size checker covers both dimensions and weight where the airline enforces a weight limit. Most US carriers don't publish a strict cabin bag weight; most non-US carriers do, ranging from 7 kg to 12 kg per bag.

Are budget airlines stricter than legacy carriers?

Significantly. Budget carriers — Ryanair, Wizz Air, Frontier, Allegiant, AirAsia — earn revenue from gate fees, so they enforce limits hard. Legacy carriers like Delta, United, BA, Lufthansa publish similar dimensions but enforce them far less frequently. Our enforcement tier breakdown above shows which group each airline belongs to.
