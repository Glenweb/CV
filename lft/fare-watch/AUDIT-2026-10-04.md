# Provenance fill — and the data audit it turned into

4 Oct 2026. Scope: the **11 airlines in the three strictest enforcement bands** — the
carriers the enforcement-index story is actually about. `source` and `lastVerified` go from
**2 of 51 to 11 of 51**.

Filling provenance meant reading each airline's published allowance and comparing it. That
found real defects.

## Verified clean — 5 of 11, exact on every figure

| Airline | Published | Dataset |
|---|---|---|
| Ryanair | 40×30×20 free; 55×40×20 / 10 kg priority | identical |
| Wizz Air | 40×30×20 / 10 kg free; 55×40×23 / 10 kg trolley | identical |
| easyJet | 45×36×20 / 15 kg small; 56×45×25 / 15 kg large | identical |
| Frontier | 18×14×8 in personal; 24×16×10 in / 35 lb carry-on | identical |
| IndiGo | 55×35×25 / 7 kg | identical |

## Two real defects — the checker is giving wrong answers

### Allegiant Air — both allowances wrong, and one costs the reader money

| | Dataset | Allegiant publishes |
|---|---|---|
| Personal item | 41 × 38 × 18 | **45 × 35 × 20** (18×14×8 in) |
| Paid carry-on | 56 × 36 × 23 | **55 × 40 × 25** (22×16×10 in) |

The carry-on width is the expensive one. The checker believes the limit is 36 cm, so it
**fails a 40 cm bag Allegiant would accept** — telling the reader to pay for a checked bag
they do not need. That is the worst failure mode a size checker has: wrong in the direction
that costs money and is never discovered, because the passenger just pays.

### Aer Lingus — the personal item is another airline's number

| | Dataset | Aer Lingus publishes |
|---|---|---|
| Small bag | 25 × 33 × 20 | **40 × 30 × 20** |
| Cabin bag | 55 × 40 × 24 / 10 kg | identical ✓ |

`25 × 33 × 20` is **flydubai's published personal item**, and it is the only triple in the
whole dataset not in descending order. It looks copied across during data entry. The
checker is rejecting compliant Aer Lingus personal items by 15 cm on the longest axis.

## Four smaller issues

| Airline | Issue |
|---|---|
| **flydubai** | Two rows, `lite_bag` and `cabin_bag`, hold identical 55×38×20 / 7 kg values. A Lite fare allowing the same bag as a standard fare is improbable — flydubai's published personal item is 25×33×20. Needs checking against the fare table. |
| **Scoot** | Second row coded `no_carry_on` at 35×25×15. Scoot publishes a personal item at 40×30×10. Both the code name and the figures look wrong. |
| **AirAsia** | Dataset has no personal item. AirAsia publishes one at 40×30×10. A missing row, not a wrong one. |
| **Vueling** | Dataset applies 10 kg to the under-seat bag. Vueling publishes 10 kg for the overhead bag only, no weight for the under-seat. Over-strict by one field. |
| **IndiGo** | Also imposes a 115 cm linear-sum limit the dataset has no field for. 55+35+25 = 115, so a bag at the stated maximum on every axis is exactly at that limit. |

## Nothing was corrected automatically. Deliberately.

`apply-provenance.py` writes `source`, `lastVerified` and `verificationMethod`, and attaches
a `dataIssue` note where there is one. **It changes no dimension.** Each defective airline
keeps its current value and gains the note.

Two reasons:
1. The figures above come from **web searches restricted to each airline's own domain**,
   which returned the official URL and quoted its content. They are not page reads.
   **Every airline domain tried is blocked by this environment's network policy** —
   allegiantair.com, aerlingus.com and flydubai.com were all refused — so the page itself
   could not be opened and checked.
2. Overwriting a published allowance on second-hand evidence is how the Aer Lingus value
   probably got there in the first place.

`verificationMethod: "official_domain_search"` records this honestly per airline, and the
data page's methodology section says the same in plain English.

### To fix the two defects

Open the two URLs, read the figures, and either confirm or correct:
- https://www.allegiantair.com/baggage-1
- https://www.aerlingus.com/prepare/bags/carry-on-baggage/

Then change `airlines.json`, re-run `python3 lft/data/build.py`,
`python3 lft/checker/tool/build-tiers.py` and `python3 lft/test-consistency.py`. The Spirit
`ceased` precedent shows the tool and page follow the data automatically.

**To let me verify directly instead:** the environment's network policy denied every
airline domain. You can widen it in the cloud environment menu in this session's title bar
→ Edit → Network access: either a broader level, or Custom with those hosts added under
Allowed domains, keeping the default package-manager list. Steps:
https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments#network-access
